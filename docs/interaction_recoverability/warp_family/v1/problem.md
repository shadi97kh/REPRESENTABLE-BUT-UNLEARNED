# Five observed laws over a known warp family — v1

**PROVED HERE — theorem scope, with proofs in the companion documents.** For every fixed known pair
\[
\theta\in[1/2,2],\qquad 0<\delta\le\delta_*:=1/3{,}200{,}000,
\]
the five-law experiment below admits one specified source-data interaction estimator with a pointwise asymptotically linear, root-\(N\) expansion. Both profiles range over the full original class and all four true coefficients range over the original box. No neighborhood of the exponential profiles is imposed. This is a family of experiments indexed by **known** warps; it is not an unknown-warp theorem. The sufficient small-separation interval does not include the historical \(\delta=.1\) example and is not a necessary restriction.

## Experiment and target

The known Gaussian latent law is \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), and
\[
h(z)=z+\delta s_\theta(z),\qquad s_\theta(z)=z\sqrt{1+\theta z^2}.
\]
There are exactly five independent response lists, \(Y_{ai}=F_a(Z_{ai})\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), \(a=0,\ldots,4\), with \(N=5n\). All latent draws are independent across observations and unobserved. Within a response the two summands share its latent draw. There is no observed cross-action pairing. The increasing latent-coordinate quantile curves are
\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(z)&=g_1(z)+g_2(h(z)),\\
F_i(z)&=g_1(z+\alpha_i)+g_2(h(z)+\beta_i),\quad i=1,2,\\
F_3(z)&=g_1(z+1)+g_2(h(z)),\\
F_4(z)&=g_1(z)+g_2(h(z)+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Private unit loadings and their component labels are known. Each unknown profile belongs to
\[
\mathcal G=\{g:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty):g\text{ real analytic},\ g(0)=1,\ g(-\infty)=0,
\tfrac12e^x\le g'(x)\le2e^x,\ |g''(x)|,|g'''(x)|\le10e^x\}.
\]
The true coefficient vector in pair order is \(c=(\alpha_1,\beta_1,\alpha_2,\beta_2)\in[.5,1]^4\). The estimator searches \([.4,1.1]^2\) for each pair; this enlarges its computational domain and keeps every original truth away from that domain's boundary. It does not enlarge or shrink the declared truth class.

For \(\Delta_{u,v}g(x)=g(x+u+v)-g(x+u)-g(x+v)+g(x)\), the unchanged target and loss are
\[
I_{12}=E\Delta_{\alpha_1,\alpha_2}g_1(Z)+E\Delta_{\beta_1,\beta_2}g_2(h(Z)),
\qquad \ell(\widehat I,I)=(\widehat I-I)^2.
\]
The joint-action response law is unavailable to the estimator. Choosing squared-error loss does not turn the proved CLT into an MSE bound.

## Quantifiers and admissible paths

**PROVED HERE.** The [attainment result](attainment.md) holds at each fixed \((\delta,\theta,g_1,g_2,c)\) in the above set. Its constants may diverge as \(\delta\downarrow0\). It does not assert a uniform CLT over the profile class or along a sequence \(\delta_N\to0\).

For local regularity, retain actual admissible paths through the truth: analytic profile directions \(r_j(0)=0\), \(|r_j^{(k)}(x)|\le C_v e^x\), \(k=0,1,2,3\), and finite coefficient directions, with the path remaining in the original restrictions. At an interior truth any such fixed direction admits a sufficiently small two-sided linear path. At a boundary truth only paths actually admitted by the constraints are asserted. Pointwise asymptotic linearity does not require the truth to be interior. No unrestricted two-sided boundary perturbation or arbitrary changing-path regularity is presumed.

Use the original Hilbert completion of admissible analytic directions under
\[
\|v\|_X^2=|d_c|^2+\sum_{j=1}^2\sum_{k=0}^3\int |r_j^{(k)}(x)|^2\frac{e^{-2x}}{1+x^2}\,dx.
\]
The score closure means the closure of scores generated by those admissible directions, not a finite dictionary. A component influence is measured in \(L^2_0(P_a)\); the stratified norm is \(\|f\|_\pi^2=\sum_a\pi_aE_af_a^2\), \(\pi_a=1/5\).

## Public information versus analysis objects

The estimator knows \(\delta,\theta,h,h^{-1},\Phi\), the private shifts, normalizations, coefficient search box and all schedules. Its inputs are the five observed lists and their common size. It does not receive true profiles, coefficients, derivatives, latent values, joint outcomes, target labels, calibration Jacobians or influence values. True Jacobians and influences appear only in the proof.

The mathematical statistic is defined for exact observations. The optional arithmetic realization in [attainment.md](attainment.md) explicitly requires refinable observation digits and certified evaluations of known parameters and primitives. It does not cover arbitrary fixed-resolution measurements. No backend or sampled-data interface is implemented in this continuation.
