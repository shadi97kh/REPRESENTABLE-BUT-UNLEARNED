# Additional reduction and source-access notes

The source comparison is limited to what was inspected. Exact algebraic reductions below are derived directly from the implemented equations; they do not depend on inaccessible theorems.

[Donoho's Statistical Estimation and Optimal Recovery (1994)](https://web.stanford.edu/dept/statistics/cgi-bin/donoho/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/SEOR.pdf) treats a linear functional of an object in a convex class, with a linear observation operator and Gaussian noise, and relates affine risk to an optimal-recovery modulus. The primary source extract was available; the full browser fetch timed out. Our finite linear fixture is in that setting after whitening. The actual nonlinear five-law model and its original parameter class are not automatically a Gaussian linear experiment or a convex image. Neither the adaptive quotient nor a local covariance matrix proves such an equivalence. No exact minimax constant is imported from the unavailable full text.

[Murphy and van der Vaart's On Profile Likelihood (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2000.10474219) studies semiparametric profile-likelihood quadratic expansions involving efficient score/information. The publisher abstract was accessible, but a direct page fetch returned 403; the exact full theorem conditions were not audited here. It establishes that profiling nuisance functions is prior art, not that this weakly identified nonlinear interaction automatically has a valid quadratic likelihood or chi-square interval. A direct-target optimizer need not reconstruct every latent component. The pilot supplies an equally informed approximate DKW-set target control and labels its incomplete optimization/sieve guarantees.

Nonlinear inverse regularization is visible directly in the objective: source distribution discrepancy is minimized over constrained functions, and a regularized local inverse score operator corrects the target. Our local finite-space bias/variance calculation is therefore an application of inverse regularization. A distinct theorem would need a quantitative, statistically attainable nonlinear target modulus, a controlled learned-space approximation, or a similar model-specific result. Recasting a nuisance error as a named remainder does not establish it.

The goal-oriented reduced-basis PDF was eventually retrieved under the new resource wrapper and pinned by SHA-256, after browser-only access failures. Its discussion distinguishes asymptotic output-error approximations from rigorous upper bounds, and it uses an available PDE/full-order model in offline construction. The detailed algorithm comparison is consolidated in `novelty.md`; it supplies no observed-data sampling rate for this project.
